# SnapList — Product Positioning

_Working note. Captures the current strategic framing so feature work stays aligned._

## One-line positioning

SnapList is an **eBay selling autopilot** — not a "listing tool." The value is the
whole loop, not any single step:

> price → list → answer buyers → close

## Why this framing

"Snap a photo, get a listing" is now table stakes — marketplaces and several tools
do it. Competing as another AI lister is a race we don't win. The defensible
position is owning the **end-to-end sale** for a seller: the part where the item
actually moves with less of their time.

The unit of value is **"an item sold with less work,"** not "a listing made."
Benchmark against _hiring an assistant to run my resale_, not against other listers.

## What to lean into (our two most ownable strengths)

1. **Pricing confidence** — sold-comps + a confidence score. The pitch is "price it
   _right_," not just "price it fast." This is where free/in-platform tools are
   weakest.
2. **Buyer-conversation automation** — drafting and handling buyer Q&A through to
   close. The least-contested part of the loop; promote it from a feature to a
   headline differentiator.

## What to hold off on

- **Full cross-listing automation.** It's the proven willingness-to-pay in this
  market but a long arms race against entrenched players. eBay-direct + export packs
  is a fine beachhead; just never marketed as a strength.
- **Net-new features.** The current feature set is enough to launch and validate.
  The near-term gap is positioning and depth, not surface area.

## Beachhead

eBay-first. Consider narrowing _marketing focus_ (not the product) to one
hard-to-price category to win a tight community before expanding. Let real sellers
confirm the niche and the lead hook before locking it.

## Known structural risk

The middleman-server position (sitting between seller and eBay) is both the moat and
the dependency — we live by eBay's API and policy surface.
